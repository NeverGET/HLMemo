## Strengths
- The database has no host ports at all and sits only on an `internal: true` network. check_tls.py verifies this against the live containers (compose.prod.yaml:49-50,142-146; check_tls.py:22-30).
- The Caddy path allowlist is exact and closed by default, with `flush_interval -1` on /mcp, a 2 MB body limit and security headers. Negative controls such as `/mcp/`, `/admin` and `/ready/extra` are tested (Caddyfile:26-42; check_tls.py:33-39).
- deploy.sh checks its inputs carefully: regex validation, `%q` remote quoting, `StrictHostKeyChecking=yes`, `flock`, pinning to an immutable SHA, and refusing a dirty checkout (deploy.sh:15-62).
- Restore fails closed: it validates the dump before stopping anything, takes and validates a safety dump, and restores with `--single-transaction --exit-on-error`. Writers stay stopped on any failure (restore.sh:15-50).
- Backups are published atomically: temp file, `pg_restore --list` check, then `mv`. The 7 daily / 4 weekly logic is correct for regular timer runs (backup.sh:18-28; retention.py:18-26).
- Terraform refuses empty or `/0` SSH CIDRs and keeps HCLOUD_TOKEN out of tfvars. The destructive drill has four independent guards (variables.tf:37-40; drill_backup_restore.sh:11-36).

## Overall
Not as-is. The core stack and TLS layout are sound, but a deploy-time backup failure causes a self-inflicted outage (#1), retention can delete the pre-upgrade dump that the rollback procedure depends on (#2), and IPv6/AAAA breaks ACME (#3). All three are small fixes and should land before the first public VPS deploy.
