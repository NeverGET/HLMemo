## Strengths
- The body is fully bounded and buffered before any pool lease. `replay_receive` keeps downstream semantics intact, and unauthenticated `/devices/register` never touches the DB.
- The namespace-3 advisory gate queues new share readers behind an exclusive revoke waiter, which fixes row-lock reader barging. `resolve` re-checks token and status after the wait.
- Commit happens and the connection is released before the first byte: finite responses are committed and returned to the pool before being sent (slow readers pin nothing). SSE commits and releases at the headers and disarms the deadline.
- F13: a uniform E_NOT_FOUND for items, `expected_versions` and card, evaluated before write-grant, replay and head checks. Grant errors are deferred until every target is visible, so there is no order oracle.
- Shielded, repeat-cancellation-safe cleanup that closes rather than pools connections possibly still used by MCP workers, and clears `state["conn"]` so the fallback cannot open a fresh transaction.

## Overall
Not as-is. F13 and G-B mostly land, but a request flood can keep revocation from ever completing (#1), a device-private project card permanently blocks other devices' card updates (#2), and the 4 MiB / 10 s body cap rejects requests the contract accepts (#3). All three need small, targeted fixes first.
